# Choose the Business Model, Go-to-Market Wedge, and Expansion Thesis

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 02, 04, 07, 08

## Question

How should the venture charge, acquire its first design partners, integrate into existing workflows, quantify ROI, cross the trust and procurement barrier, expand beyond its initial India-first wedge, and defend itself as models and incumbent features improve?

## Answer

**Category and promise.** PayoutProof is the control layer for high-risk payout exceptions. It converts scattered request evidence into an exact Payment Intent, reviewable Risk Case, and required safe next step before the existing approval rail. It sells faster, more consistent exception handling—not generic fraud prevention, deepfake detection, compliance, or finance automation.

**Acquisition, qualification, and commitment.** Use a founder-led, account-based funnel of roughly 30 workflow-qualified businesses, 10 structured discovery conversations, and three active Design Partners, reached through warm introductions and targeted outbound. Buildathon visibility, finance communities, advisers, and rails are credibility and referral sources; broad paid acquisition waits. Each Design Partner needs a named Finance Control Owner, at least two participating Payment Operators, an existing maker-checker rail, recurring qualifying exceptions, and commitment to baseline measurement and structured feedback. Optimize for cross-sector workflow fit rather than logos or a premature vertical. Start with short, scoped, unpaid discovery requiring stakeholder and workflow access, then require a paid fixed-scope pilot or, when procurement prevents payment, an LOI or equivalent commitment.

**Pilot, pricing, economics, integration, and ROI.** Run a six-to-eight-week pilot around one workflow, one team, one customer policy, and at most one lightweight rail handoff, beginning with sanitized historical or jointly constructed cases and permitting governed live workflow observation without live-money authority. Require baseline mapping, paired cases, and weekly feedback. Convert only when safety and correctness hold, operator interactions fall at least 30%, and a production owner and budget exist; permit one short paid extension for a predeclared measurement gap, otherwise export results, delete governed data, and stop. Test a fixed ₹1–2 lakh pilot fee with 50% credited toward the first annual contract. Production uses an annual platform subscription with Payment Operator and Risk Case-volume bands plus one standard rail integration; ₹6–12 lakh ARR is a willingness-to-pay hypothesis, not validated pricing. Additional integrations, deployment constraints, enterprise support, and premium detectors are separately priced. Do not charge on protected value, promise prevented loss, or impose per-case tolls that encourage bypass. Keep onboarding bounded to one policy mapping and one standard integration, cap founder effort around 20–30 hours per pilot, target at least 75% software gross margin after the design-partner phase, and reject managed review or non-reusable customization. Land as a rail-neutral overlay using operator-submitted evidence and minimal read-only context; initially export or attach the Risk Case, adding one human-initiated pending-payout handoff only after immutable-intent safety holds. The primary ROI measure is paired operator interactions per qualifying exception, with at least a 30% reduction and no Unsafe Handoff or policy-correctness degradation; report time, evidence completeness, unnecessary holds and step-ups, overrides, and modeled exposure separately, never as realized ROI.

**Assurance, red lines, and data rights.** Discovery uses synthetic or sanitized evidence plus an assurance pack covering data flow, purpose limitation, isolation, encryption, retention/deletion, subprocessors and model providers, access, audit logging, incident contact, and absence of Money Action authority. Paid pilots add a DPA, named processing purposes, deletion verification, security responses, and an exit/export path. Certifications and customer-managed deployment are demand-driven production-hardening milestones, never implied credentials. Refuse autonomous Money Actions, maker-checker bypass, unnecessary broad access, indefinite retention, default raw-data training, unsupported identity, ownership, fraud, or compliance claims, transfer of reusable core IP, and premature bespoke on-premises support. Customers own their evidence, policies, outputs, and identifiable operational data; PayoutProof owns its generic schemas, software, evaluations, connectors, and contractually permitted de-identified learnings. Raw evidence, Payment Intents, customer policies, and identifiable reviewer activity remain customer-isolated. De-identified operational telemetry requires explicit rights, while evaluation or model-improvement contributions require separate opt-in permission; pooled raw evidence is not the moat.

**Defensibility.** Own the request-level Risk Case, immutable Payment Intent binding, deterministic Policy Gate contract, customer-specific reviewer history, reproducible safety evaluation, and rail-neutral integrations. Detectors and foundation models remain replaceable. The compounding loop is trusted deployments to better policy and exception patterns, faster and safer implementation, stronger evaluation evidence, and easier procurement and distribution.

**Expansion and gates.** Expand first to adjacent controls owned by the same Finance Control Owner, beginning with beneficiary-master and bank-detail changes and then other high-risk finance exceptions; next add demanded rail and ERP integrations, then larger enterprises and regulated institutions, and finally validated international markets. Do not expand into generic fraud orchestration, consumer scam detection, or a payment rail. At least two of three Design Partners must pass the workflow and safety gates and convert or commit before adding an adjacent workflow. Add a connector only after demand from two qualified customers or one funded channel. Pursue non-exclusive rail or ERP referral, co-sell, or integration partnerships after two successful paid pilots without surrendering the customer relationship or control contract. Enter regulated institutions only after several stable production customers, and another geography only after the schema survives multiple integrations and a local buyer, policy regime, and channel are validated.

**Uncertainty.** Buyer willingness to pay, qualifying-case frequency, conversion rates, price elasticity, production ROI, and the best eventual vertical remain customer-discovery hypotheses rather than established facts.
